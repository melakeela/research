Institutional identity to inherit
   * MelaKeela is the public museum experience.
   * Veḷi is the threshold, evidence field and organizing concept beneath and around it. The exact public phrasing still requires consistent resolution across homepage, About, evidence pages and institutional materials.
   * The record is the unit of accountability. The investigation is the unit of experience. Entering a proposition into the ledger does not make it true.
   * Connection is not continuity. Bridges are claims. Every proposed relationship needs evidence, mechanism, chronology, alternatives and status.
   * PROVE IT is the signature reasoning interaction: the museum states a bounded claim, exposes evidence and complication, lets the visitor reason, then accounts for its own conclusion.
   * Children are not given a false simplified history. They enter the same evidence through different actions and language.
Mandatory research method
Apply the following to every item and every historical claim:
   1. Chronological gating before narrative space. A historical hypothesis must first fit the relevant date and place. A model that fails that gate receives only enough space to document its exclusion or historiographical importance.
   2. Proportionality. Space and prominence track evidentiary weight, not controversy, citation volume or a desire to appear balanced. Unequal hypotheses do not receive symmetric treatment.
   3. Source ancestry. Several modern sources repeating one study, translation or excavation report are not independent confirmation. Record the upstream observation and dependency chain.
   4. Exact evidence roles. Distinguish what supports, contradicts, dates, localizes, identifies, contextualizes, suggests or cannot discriminate.
   5. Original evidence first. Prefer primary texts, critical editions, excavation reports, object records, datasets and original linguistic/genetic studies. Record what was actually opened and the exact locator.
   6. Separate evidence domains. Language, ancestry, archaeological culture, material movement, polity, religion, caste and modern identity do not inherit certainty from one another.
   7. Separate dates. Event, composition, redaction, attestation, manuscript, excavation, analysis, publication and modern interpretation are different dates.
   8. Absence protocol. Distinguish not produced, not preserved, not excavated, not sampled, not published, inaccessible, unrecognized and documented destruction. Absence becomes informative only when the expected trace and sampling context are adequate.
   9. Decolonize. Reopen colonial classifications, translations, racial theory, excavation priorities, museum custody and textbook inheritance; identify who funded, collected, classified, translated, withheld and published.
   10. Debrahminize. Distinguish Sanskrit/Brahmanical preservation from authorship or origin; investigate vernacular, oral, agrarian, artisanal, women’s, subordinated and non-Brahmanical evidence without assigning every unknown contribution to a preferred identity.
   11. De-Indo-Europeanize civilizational credit. Retain well-supported linguistic genealogy while testing contact, post-PIE innovation, BMAC/Oxus input, South Asian areal change and extinct-language contribution feature by feature.
   12. Audit every nationalism. Test Hindu-nationalist, Tamil/Dravidian-nationalist, Iranian-nationalist, Greek/European-first, Afrocentric and other priority narratives by the same chronological and evidentiary rules. Equal method does not mean equal historical power or equal explanatory standing.
   13. Community evidence without fossilization. Living Brahui, Kurukh, Malto, Tamil, Adivasi or other communities are not linguistic fossils. Community accounts may be authoritative for living identity and knowledge but do not automatically date ancient continuity.
   14. The system must be capable of contradicting MelaKeela’s preferred hypothesis. Otherwise Veḷi becomes advocacy infrastructure rather than research infrastructure.
Status dimensions
Never collapse these:
   * Epistemic: ESTABLISHED / SUPPORTED / ARGUED / OPEN / CONTESTED / REJECTED / UNVERIFIED.
   * Editorial: DRAFT / UNDER REVIEW / REVIEWED / REVISED.

A claim may simultaneously be `CONTESTED · REVIEWED · LIVE`. Those labels answer different questions.

The 89 work items
I. Truth layer — do now
1. Audit every load-bearing factual claim
Inherited work: Earlier curatorial audits assigned risk across historical snapshots of 96, 105, 108 and later reported 133 routes/pages. Those counts cannot be treated as the same unit or as the current inventory. Linguistic and Rigvedic packets demonstrated why apparently sourced prose can still depend on one upstream scholar or unrun corpus search.

Gate: Every high-impact live claim is traceable or placed on correction/research hold. Do not attempt equal-depth review of every minor sentence first.
2. Fix numerical inconsistencies
Inherited work: Site-size figures have repeatedly shifted because pages, routes, redirects, records, claims, sources, sites and exhibits were conflated. Research packets also circulated irreproducible totals for loanwords, dānastutis and fort passages.
Build out: Establish a canonical metrics dictionary and one generated source for public counts. Every number must name its denominator, date, query and inclusion rule. Historical audit counts remain labelled snapshots.
Gate: No public metric is manually duplicated or presented without definition; unresolved research totals are attributed or withheld.
3. Establish one status vocabulary
Inherited work: “Provisional,” “under review,” “contested,” “live” and “in development” have been used as though interchangeable. Project files already require separate epistemic, editorial, publication, product and access dimensions.
Build out: Publish a controlled vocabulary, transition rules, responsible role and examples. Map all legacy labels without silently upgrading them.
Gate: A status linter or review table can identify invalid combinations and every public badge has one unambiguous meaning.
4. Formalize the epistemic constitution
Inherited work: Core rules already exist: connection is not continuity; similarity is not descent; ancestry is not language; reconstruction is not attestation; inscriptions do not prove mass literacy; living practice does not automatically prove ancient survival; bridges are claims; absence needs a sampling context.
Build out: Consolidate these into `METHODOLOGY-CONSTITUTION.md`, add examples from MelaKeela’s actual failures, specify correction authority and require the system to contradict preferred hypotheses where warranted.
Gate: Every template, research packet and relationship type can be tested against the constitution; it is public-facing but not repetitive boilerplate on every page.
5. Rewrite overdetermined headlines
Inherited work: The project wants wit, brevity and force—not dull academic hedging—but dramatic conclusions have sometimes outrun evidence. Stronger models include THE 99 FORTS — What exactly did Indra’s poets claim to destroy?, THE BLUE ROAD, THE LANGUAGE THAT REFUSED TO DISAPPEAR, and THE SAME WORLD, TWO RESPONSES.
Build out: Audit high-risk headlines and restructure as `provocative question → bounded finding → evidence → complication`. Preserve memorability without turning hypotheses into verdicts.
Gate: A visitor can tell what is known, argued and open from the opening screen without reading a legalistic disclaimer wall.
6. Separate evidence from historiography
Inherited work: MelaKeela repeatedly asks both what happened in antiquity and how colonial, nationalist, Brahmanical, university and museum institutions later narrated it. Those questions have sometimes blurred.
Build out: Every major investigation separates `ANCIENT QUESTION`, `HISTORIOGRAPHICAL QUESTION` and, when relevant, `KNOWLEDGE-POLITICS QUESTION`. Connect them through typed claims rather than merging them.
Gate: Criticism of a scholar’s politics cannot substitute for testing the ancient evidence, and ancient evidence cannot erase the institutional history of its interpretation.
7. Audit the homepage
Inherited work: The homepage is meant to be a restrained threshold, not an About page or card catalogue. Earlier inventories found missing metadata and competing entrance functions; later counts are not current until rechecked.
Build out: Verify the current homepage’s factual statements, CTA, accessibility, canonical domain, metadata, performance and relationship to `/enter`, Search and Research Index. Preserve the sense that the past left evidence, not finished answers.
Gate: In seconds, visitors understand that MelaKeela is a museum they can investigate and Veḷi makes the underlying evidence inspectable.
8. Lock the MelaKeela / Veḷi relationship
Inherited work: The stable core is MelaKeela as the public museum and Veḷi as threshold/evidence field. Past documents alternately call Veḷi the infrastructure, organizing concept or institutional identity. This inconsistency remains consequential.
Build out: Decide the public one-sentence relationship, naming hierarchy, wordmark use and propagation rules for About, footer, methodology, repository and institutional materials. Do not force the same explanation onto every visitor screen.
Gate: One approved naming architecture exists and all high-level surfaces use it consistently.
II. Research programmes — research first
9. Comparative Dravidian programme
Inherited work: The programme must move beyond Tamil/Sanskrit binaries and include Tamil, Malayalam, Kannada, Telugu, Tulu, Kodava, Gondi, Kui/Kuwi, Kurukh, Malto, Brahui and relevant lesser-documented languages. Prior substrate work exposed misuse of DEDR entries as proof of borrowing.
Build out: Create inspectable cognate dossiers distinguishing attested form, reconstruction, regular correspondence, borrowing, proposed borrowing, semantic parallel, chance resemblance and disputed etymology. Include subgroup distribution and direction-of-borrowing tests.
Gate: Visitors can inspect why linguists relate forms; no isolated resemblance or dictionary cross-reference is promoted into ancestry or borrowing.
10. Brahui / Kurukh / Malto investigation
Inherited work: Four models must be tested: long-term retention, later migration, complex migration plus language shift/contact, and other specialist models. The owner’s highland-refugia insight, late Balochi expansion, modern genetic assimilation and absence of a documented medieval migration are central questions—not automatic proof of Bronze Age continuity.
Build out: Reconstruct loan layers, Balochi chronology, community histories, elevation/state-access patterns, ancient-DNA limits, toponymy and predicted evidence. Build 500-year language maps of Balochistan with `ATTESTED / INFERRED / POSSIBLE / UNKNOWN` polygons.
Gate: Models are ranked by evidence and predictions, not forced into false equality; living peoples are not treated as fossils; Brahui is not called proof of the Indus language.
11. Indus language programme
Inherited work: Dravidian interpretations deserve serious treatment, but undeciphered means undeciphered. The project rejects both default “Language X” shielding and isolated rebus certainty.
Build out: Separate sign structure, corpus statistics, archaeological context, language-family hypothesis, proposed readings, iconography and later linguistic evidence. Test Dravidian and serious alternatives using repeatability, sign distribution, grammar prediction and falsifiers.
Gate: No proposed reading becomes an established record without reproducible performance across unseen inscriptions and specialist scrutiny.
12. Indo-Iranian / Iranian chronology programme
Inherited work: Questions include earliest Indo-Iranian evidence, BMAC/Oxus contact, Iranian presence around Balochistan, Vedic/Avestan divergences, pre-Zoroastrian ritual, Zaraθuštra’s historicity/date and the difference between Iranian empire and local speech.
Build out: Create synchronized archaeological, linguistic, textual, genetic and political chronologies. Reconstruct common ritual vocabulary without turning a loan layer into wholesale religious origin. Keep Old and Younger Avestan distinct.
Gate: “Proto-Indo-Iranian,” “Proto-Iranian,” “Avestan,” “Old Persian,” “Saka” and “Balochi” are never used interchangeably.
13. Proto-Dravidian phonology instrument
Inherited work: Key public questions include reconstructed *c, lack of a secure native */s/ series, the retroflex coronal system and scholarly *ẓ as a retroflex continuant rather than ordinary /z/. Tamil ழ is a major descendant case.
Build out: Map reconstruction symbol → proposed phonetic realization → daughter outcomes → attested examples → sources → disputes. Distinguish phoneme, phone, grapheme and transliteration.
Gate: Every reconstruction is visibly reconstructed; no chart equates PIE contextual [z] with Proto-Dravidian *ẓ.
14. Tamil textual corpus parity
Inherited work: A checkpoint found an extreme infrastructure imbalance—hundreds of Sanskrit texts against one Tamil text. MelaKeela cannot critique archive asymmetry while reproducing it computationally.
Build out: Establish editions, rights and searchable corpora for early Tamil texts and inscriptions with lexical search, named entities, geography, social vocabulary, material culture, labour, ecology, ritual and political terminology. Preserve original, transliteration, gloss and translation separately.
Gate: Major Tamil-based claims can be reproduced at passage level with infrastructure comparable in function—not necessarily identical in size—to the Sanskrit lane.
15. Pāli / Prakrit / Jain corpus expansion
Inherited work: The śramaṇa archive must be first-class evidence, especially for Greater Magadha, ethics, karma/rebirth, linguistic change, social categories and what Brahmanical corpora preserve or omit.
Build out: Select critical corpora, date witnesses and linguistic varieties, and establish passage-level comparisons across Buddhist, Jain and Brahmanical materials without treating “Pāli” or “Prakrit” as a single vernacular moment.
Gate: Large claims about absence, invention or diffusion cannot rely on Sanskrit versus modern summary alone.
16. Aśokan inscription corpus
Inherited work: This is a flagship candidate because it unites place, script, language, ruler, audience and regional variation. It also anchors later claims about Brāhmī and political communication.
Build out: Provide image/record, original, transliteration, translation, variants, dating basis, location, term search and scholarship for each inscription. Map dhamma terms and Brahmana/Śramaṇa references without assuming uniform reception.
Gate: Every displayed reading points to an inscription and edition; regional differences remain visible.
17. Chinese pilgrim corpus
Inherited work: Faxian, Xuanzang, Yijing and related travelers can connect passage, itinerary, institution, landscape and chronology, but later accounts cannot be projected backward uncritically.
Build out: Model route segment, travel date, text/witness date, named institution, observation type, translation and archaeological comparison. Mark hearsay and later manuscript distance.
Gate: The map distinguishes where the traveler went, what was reported, and what modern archaeology can or cannot corroborate.
18. Archaeology source programme
Inherited work: Priority sites include Keezhadi, Adichanallur, Kodumanal, Arikamedu, Dholavira, Rakhigarhi, Harappa, Mohenjo-daro and relevant South Indian megalithic/Iron Age sites. Earlier pages sometimes jumped from object to civilization-wide conclusion.
Build out: Record excavator, trench/context, layer, object/sample, association, dating method, report, later reinterpretation, repository and rights. Prioritize primary excavation publications over press summaries.
Gate: A site claim can be traced to context and dating evidence; unexcavated and unpublished areas remain visible unknowns.
19. Genetics evidence standard
Inherited work: Project discussions include ancestry formation, caste/endogamy, Brahui, Munda chronology and Y-lineage claims. Prior outputs sometimes confused uniparental markers, autosomal ancestry, language and population history.
Build out: Require sample, context, date, laboratory, coverage, contamination, model, comparator populations, uncertainty, paper and interpretive limit. Re-audit `the-three-ancestries`, `the-genome-of-caste`, Brahui and substrate-related pages.
Gate: No genetic component or haplogroup is labelled a language, caste, ethnicity or culture without independent bridging evidence.
III. Evidence architecture — specify before building
20. Universal Evidence Object
Inherited work: The project has a draft field set for record identity, evidence class, date, place, context, provenance, repository, sources, interpretations, uncertainty, rights, claims and revision history. Earlier P1–P3 work preserved the concept but implementation was rejected as premature.
Build out: Produce a minimal extensible schema with examples from an artifact, inscription, text passage, genetic sample and linguistic reconstruction. Do not bury evidence only inside page prose.
Gate: The schema represents real pilot cases without pretending unlike evidence classes share identical fields.
21. Claims become first-class objects
Inherited work: A public sentence must have identity, author, date, support, contradiction, context, assessment, alternatives and change conditions. The ledger is not merely a bibliography.
Build out: Define claim versioning, scope, responsible author/reviewer, downstream routes and public wording. Separate claim truth assessment from publication status.
Gate: One correction can identify every page, visualization and relationship affected.
22. Relationships become first-class objects
Inherited work: “Bridges are claims” is the most distinctive Veḷi principle. Required relation types already include genealogy, borrowing, transmission, analogy, shared source, contact possible and disputed.
Build out: Store endpoints, relationship type, proposition, mechanism, chronology, evidence, counter-evidence, status, strength dimensions, author and revisions. Avoid one decorative confidence score.
Gate: Every visible line in an Atlas or contribution graph can be opened and defended; uncertain edges can stop, break or compete.
23. Evidence roles
Inherited work: The programme already names SUPPORTS, CONTRADICTS, CONTEXTUALIZES, DATES, LOCALIZES, IDENTIFIES, SUGGESTS and CANNOT DISCRIMINATE.
Build out: Define each role, allowable evidence classes and examples. Let one record play different roles for different claims.
Gate: “Source attached” is no longer treated as “claim supported”; the argumentative work is explicit.
24. Revision history
Inherited work: Public correction is intended as a feature, not an embarrassment. Earlier erroneous counts and substrate framing show why inherited claims need visible revision.
Build out: Define version events, reason, evidence change, reviewer, effective date, previous wording and downstream propagation. Design visitor-facing summaries such as “new chronology weakened the earlier connection.”
Gate: Significant corrections are preserved, queryable and propagated rather than overwritten silently.
IV. Flagship experiences
25. Artifact Atlas v2
Inherited work: The Atlas is the FIELD engine: `PLACE × TIME × RECORD × UNCERTAINTY`. A dedicated language-movement mode must distinguish artifacts, texts, reconstructed stages, ancestry movement, political control and unknown speech zones.
Build out: Support filters for evidence class, dating confidence, excavated/unexcavated, attested/reconstructed, documented/inferred route and claims using a record. Include the Blue Road, Red Road, Hormuz and a rigorously sourced language-change slider only when records are ready.
Gate: Clicking a site explains whether it supplies linguistic evidence, a possible contact setting or no language evidence. No arrow silently identifies a people or language.
26. PROVE IT
Inherited work: This is the signature public interaction and should be demonstrated with one bounded case before generalization. It must reveal MelaKeela’s reasoning and allow genuine disagreement.
Build out: Use `CLAIM → WHAT WAS FOUND → WHY THIS READING → COMPLICATION → UNKNOWN → WHAT WOULD CHANGE IT → YOU DECIDE → MELAKEELA’S READING → WHY WE MAY DISAGREE`. Candidate pilots include Keezhadi literacy, a Dravidian etymology or a fort interpretation only after source readiness.
Gate: The visitor’s conclusion is not gamified as right/wrong; every offered conclusion is constrained by the evidence packet.
27. WATER
Inherited work: WATER is the strongest proposed first Living World, connecting Dholavira, palaeoclimate, engineering, South Indian water infrastructure, Tamil sources and Chennai hydrology. The continuity belongs initially to the human problem, not automatically to one civilization.
Build out: Select a bounded route with dated records, material mechanisms, discontinuities and modern stakes. Use Bhavani Raman’s Chennai water work and relevant archaeological/environmental scholarship where appropriate.
Gate: Gaps remain gaps; ancient-to-modern juxtaposition does not become an unsupported chain of cultural continuity.

Cross-cutting programme architecture
The 89 numbered items must also be reconciled into six programmes. These are not six additional backlog numbers:
   1. INDUS: urbanism, writing, language hypotheses, trade, water, transformation and population.
   2. DRAVIDIAN: comparative linguistics, archaeology, Brahui/Kurukh/Malto, Tamil, material vocabulary and contact.
   3. TEXT AND POWER: Rigveda, Avestan, śramaṇa traditions, Tamil corpus, Aśoka, manuscript transmission and knowledge institutions.
   4. PEOPLE AND MOVEMENT: archaeology, genetics, language, migration, pastoralism, trade and population formation.
   5. LIVING WORLDS: Water, Food and Language.
   6. HISTORIES OF KNOWLEDGE: colonial archaeology, museums, philology, classification, nationalist historiography, translation, preservation and exclusion.
