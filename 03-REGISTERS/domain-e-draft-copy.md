## QUESTION

When the Rigveda was composed in the northwest, whose languages were
already being spoken there — and how much of the answer can the surviving
evidence actually carry?

## WHAT IS OBSERVED

R. L. Turner's comparative dictionary of the Indo-Aryan languages marks
loans with an arrow. Counted directly: **224 entries take a Dravidian
source, 55 take a Munda source**, and in ten entries the arrow points the
other way, out of Indo-Aryan. (`DE-M-002`, `DE-M-003`, `DE-M-006`)

Of those 279 attributions, **fifteen words occur in the Rigveda as
independent lemmas** — twenty-one occurrences in a corpus of 164,758.
Thirteen-thousandths of one percent. (`DE-M-009` to `DE-M-011`)

Where those fifteen do occur, ten of the twenty-one occurrences sit in
verses Arnold's metrical stratification calls Popular, against a
corpus-wide share of fourteen percent. (`DE-M-012`)

On the map, the nearest Dravidian language to the northwest is **Brahui
in Balochistan, 393 km away**. The nearest Munda language is **Korku,
855 km away**. (`DE-M-023`, `DE-M-024`)

## WHAT THE EVIDENCE SUPPORTS

Dravidian and the various proposed unattested donors are not competing
answers of the same kind. Behind Dravidian stand 76,303 recorded reflexes
under 5,619 dictionary entries, a set of reconstructed proto-forms, and
an independently built cognate-coded sample of twenty varieties. Behind
Para-Munda, the Kubhā-Vipāś prefixing language and Masica's Language X
stand arguments — which may be good arguments, and are a different kind
of object. (`DE-I-002`, `DE-M-013` to `DE-M-017`)

A Munda language proper is a poor candidate for the northwestern donor.
No attested Munda language falls anywhere near the northwest, and the
Munda range begins east of where the northwestern languages end.
(`HYP-E-002`)

That is a finding about **Munda proper only.** It says nothing about
whether some other, extinct, unattested language was there. Those are
separate claims and this record keeps them separate. (`DE-I-007`)

## WHAT COMPLICATES IT

**Dravidian's whole position in the northwest is one language.** Take
Brahui out and the nearest Dravidian language is farther from the
northwest than the nearest Munda language is. Whether Brahui is the last
survivor of an older northwestern Dravidian, or arrived late from
elsewhere, is genuinely disputed and is not settled here.
(`DE-M-025`, `DE-I-008`)

**The 224-to-55 ratio measures a dictionary, not an era.** It was
compiled in Britain in the 1960s, and Dravidian lexicography available to
its compiler was considerably better than Munda lexicography. That alone
could produce the gap. (`DE-I-004`, `APA-E-002`)

**The Munda attributions are close to one person's.** Eighty-five percent
of them cite F. B. J. Kuiper. Fifty-five citations tracing to one scholar
are one opinion, not fifty-five. (`DE-M-028`)

**A liturgical corpus is a poor census of contact.** The Rigveda records
what priests composed for patrons. Everyday vocabulary — farming, craft,
household — enters only where the liturgy needed it, so the
thirteen-thousandths figure is a floor on what that genre wrote down, not
a measure of how much contact there was. (`APA-E-001`)

**The metrical dating is one scholar's, from 1905**, and the "Popular"
layer is not independent of the tenth book. The clustering is real in the
data; the step from it to a date is not taken here. (`BR-E-002`)

**Counts from the Dravidian etymological dictionary are less stable than
they look.** Two independent digitizations of the same book disagree on
about a tenth of their entries, and neither can be checked against print
from here. (`IC-E-001`)

## WHAT REMAINS UNKNOWN

Whether any language of the Dravidian family was spoken in the northwest
at the time of Rigvedic composition. The geography is consistent with it;
the geography of today is not evidence about three thousand years ago.
(`BR-E-003`)

Whether an unattested prefixing language was there. This record cannot
say — the proposals could not be retrieved from this session at all, and
that is a fact about a network policy, not about the proposals.
(`HOLD-002`)

Which of the 224 Dravidian etymologies survive individual scrutiny.
Counting a dictionary's attributions is not testing them. (`BR-E-001`)

What specialists currently think. The most recent assessment reachable
here is from 1966. (`DE-I-009`)

## MELAKEELA'S CURRENT INTERPRETATION

Dravidian is the strongest of the proposed donors, and the reason is not
that its case is proven. It is that Dravidian is an attested family with
a reconstructible history and a living northwestern outlier, while its
rivals are proposals about languages nobody has ever recorded. Those are
different kinds of claim and they do not get equal space.

But the Dravidian case as measured here is thinner than the confidence
usually attached to it, its geographic footing is a single language whose
own history is unsettled, and the strongest instrument behind it is a
sixty-year-old dictionary that had better Dravidian materials than Munda
ones.

And "unidentified" remains the honest description of part of the
residue — as a residue, never as a rival explanation.

## WHAT WOULD CHANGE IT

- Any Dravidian trace in the northwest that does not run through Brahui.
- Settling the date of Brahui's presence in Balochistan.
- Retrieving the substrate literature and gating it properly.
- A loan list built from a Dravidian-headed instrument rather than an
  Indo-Aryan-headed one.
- Evidence of Munda-family speech anywhere west of Korku, at any period.
- The apparent clustering in the latest metrical layer dissolving on a
  larger sample.
