# HOLD-008 — the metrically restored text's editorial marks have no key

**Raised by:** domain A, 2026-09-07
**Blocks:** any classification of *what kind* of restoration a marked
pāda carries. Does not block `DA-M-024` to `DA-M-028`, which count
marks and syllables.
**Ledger:** `SRC-071`

## What is held

`versions/vnh.csv` carries eight mark characters over 1,084 pāda
instances:

| mark | pādas |
|---|---|
| `+` | 510 |
| `@` | 198 |
| `'` | 125 |
| `\` | 103 |
| `*` | 89 |
| `&` | 36 |
| `~` | 19 |
| `/` | 4 |

The repository documents none of them. Their behaviour is legible in
context — at RV 1.003.10a the transmitted `pāvakā́ naḥ` appears as
`pavākā́+ naḥ`, and at 1.025.04b `vásya~iṣṭaye` restores a hiatus the
transmitted text has closed — but legible in context is not a key, and
this record does not promote a plausible reading of a symbol to a
statement about an editorial convention.

The marks are therefore **counted and not read** throughout. `DA-M-028`
reports that marked pādas are commoner in Arnold's Archaic stratum
(3.69% against 2.11–2.21% elsewhere, p = 1.4e-12) without saying what
kind of restoration is commoner there, which is the interesting
question and is the one held.

## What is needed

Van Nooten, Barend A. and Gary B. Holland, *Rig Veda: A Metrically
Restored Text with an Introduction and Notes*, Harvard Oriental Series
50, Cambridge MA 1994 — the introduction states the conventions. Or the
Thomson and Slocum presentation at the University of Texas Linguistics
Research Center, which the TEI names as the file's immediate source.

## What is *not* claimed

That the restoration is arbitrary or that its marks are noise. The
opposite: `DA-M-026` finds 96.93% of restored pādas scanning to 8, 11
or 12 syllables, which is what a disciplined restoration looks like
from outside.
