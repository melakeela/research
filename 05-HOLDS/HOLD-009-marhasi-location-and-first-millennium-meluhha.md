# HOLD-009 — Where Marhaši was, and what first-millennium Meluhha denotes

**Opened:** 2026-09-09, domain R.
**Blocks:** `HYP-R-008` (`NOT-ELIGIBLE-SOURCE-BLOCKED`); the geographical
half of `HYP-R-006`; and any corridor claim that needs the four toponyms
placed rather than merely counted.
**Typed absence:** `DR-NEG-011`, `NOT ACCESSIBLE`.

## Two questions, one missing literature

**Marhaši.** This unit measured the toponym — 153 texts, written
`mar-ha-szi{ki}` with variants `mar-ha-s,i` and `mar-ha-s,u`, 113 of them
catalogued Ur III, concentrated at Puzriš-Dagan, and the most separate of the
four in co-occurrence (`DRM-006`). It could not retrieve **where the literature
places it, or on what evidence.** The Assyriological work on the toponym,
principally Steinkeller's, is in no corpus reachable from this session.

**First-millennium Meluhha.** The corpus shows the same sign sequence used in
55 first-millennium texts, taking the land determinative `{kur}` where the
third-millennium texts take the place determinative `{ki}`, in royal itineraries
at Nineveh rather than granary receipts at Girsu, more than a millennium after
the Indus cities ended (`DRM-008`). There is a standard scholarly position on
what the later word denotes. **This unit did not retrieve it and therefore does
not state it.**

## Why the second question is stated as an absence rather than answered

Naming the first-millennium referent from unretrieved knowledge would put an
unsourced claim into the register with the authority of a correction. `BF-031`
records the same discipline applied to a date in a retrieved paper, and the
principle is the same: a well-known answer recalled is not a retrieved one.

What the measurement establishes on its own is narrower and is enough to be
useful: **continuity of reference across those 1,400 years cannot be assumed**,
and a corridor argument built on "Meluhha" must say which Meluhha it means.

## What was tried

A Scholar Gateway query of 2026-09-09T02:30Z, framed on the location of
Marhaši, the evidence for placing it near Kerman or the Halil Rud, and its
distinction from Meluhha and Elam, returned 18 passages across 13 articles. The
relevant returns were a study of gypsum alabaster beads at Konar Sandal
(`SRC-126`) and one of the Shahr-i Sokhta cemetery (`SRC-127`); neither
addresses the toponym. The remainder matched on "Kerman" and "Jiroft" and are
papers on Iranian flood frequency, herpetology, *Euphorbia* seed morphology,
phytoplasma infections of cucumber and toxoplasmosis seroprevalence.

`oracc.museum.upenn.edu`, `etcsl.orinst.ox.ac.uk` and CDLI's own front end are
refused at the gateway (`SRC-099`), so the transliterations counted here could
not be checked against edited and translated text with an apparatus.

## What would clear it

1. Access to ORACC and ETCSL, which would supply edition and translation for
   the very texts this unit has already located by P-number — the narrowest
   change, and the one with the best ratio of access granted to questions
   answered, because the corpus work is already done.
2. A literature lane reaching Assyriological monographs and journals.

Either would also let `HYP-R-006`'s geographical gate be assessed rather than
recorded as unassessable.

## What must not happen while it is held

Marhaši must not be collapsed into Meluhha, and neither must be placed from
memory. `DRM-018` rejects the Mehrgarh identification as an inference without
asserting any alternative, and that is the shape every statement about
Marhaši's location takes in this unit until this hold clears.
