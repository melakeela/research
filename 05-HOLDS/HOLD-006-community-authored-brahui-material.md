# HOLD-006 — community-authored Brahui material, held out of every register

**Raised by:** domain M, six-models unit, 2026-09-07, on a self-audit that
found the rule had been breached.
**Blocks:** nothing in this unit. Everything below is *unused*.
**Ledger:** `SRC-066`, `SRC-073`, `SRC-074`, `SRC-075`. **Decision:** `D-041`.

## What went wrong, and the correction

`CLAUDE.md` and the owner's instruction both say the same thing: community-authored
accounts are authoritative for living identity and knowledge, are **not**
automatically a literal Bronze Age chronology, and *where any is used, route it
to the rights steward for consent and attribution* **before it enters a
register**.

The first draft of this unit put one of them into a register anyway. `DMC-006`
in `03-REGISTERS/domain-m-balochi-chronology.csv` carried the Rudbar-Jonub
migration dates, sourced to community oral literature and named local
informants, at status `HOLD`.

The reasoning was that `HOLD` means "recorded but not used". It does not
satisfy the rule. **The rule is about the register, not about the status** — a
`HOLD` row is still a register row, still carries the content, and still
travels with the register into anything built from it. The row has been
**withdrawn from the register** and its content is held here instead, where
nothing downstream consumes it.

Two smaller instances were corrected with it. `DMI-011` cited `SRC-066` in its
notes for the existence of an Elamo-Dravidian proposal; that fact is already on
this repository's record at `DMB-024` from a non-community source, and the note
now cites that instead. Nothing else in any register cites any of the four.

## What is held

**`SRC-073` — Sheybanifard, "Word Formation in Brahui Language of
Rudbar-Jonub/Rudbari", *Al-Burz*, 2023.** Reports that a group of Brahui
speakers entered Sistan and Balochistan province in Iran from the Pakistan
borders *within the last 400 years*, and that about *200 years ago* a group
entered southern Kerman; the Rudbar-Jonub variety has fewer than 1,000
speakers and many are quadrilingual in Balochi, Brahui, Rudbari and Persian.
Sourced to community oral literature and named local informants. **This is the
row that was withdrawn.**

**`SRC-074` — Bano et al., "Impact of Globalization on Brahui language",
*Al-Burz*, 2016.** Reports Brahui endangerment and lexical replacement, and
rehearses without adjudicating the competing origin accounts that circulate
*within the community itself* — Aryan, Turko-Iranian, Dravidian-with-Mohenjo-daro,
and a Balochi–Dravidian fusion narrative.

**`SRC-066` — McAlpin, "Modern Colloquial Eastern Elamite", *Al-Burz*, 2022,**
and **`SRC-075` — "Similarities and Unique Homogeneity of Certain Letters of
Brahui and Balochi Language", *Makhz*, 2024,** which attributes its convergence
position to the Brahui linguist Abdul Razzaq Sabir. These are a different case
from the first two and the difference is for the rights steward to confirm, not
for this file to decide: they are scholars publishing in community-hosted
venues, so the question is attribution and venue access rather than consent to
use community knowledge. They are held with the others until that is confirmed.

## Two things that must not happen to `SRC-073` when it is released

**It is not a Bronze Age chronology.** It documents a *recent, local* movement
and is authoritative for that and for living identity. Reading oral history
about the last four centuries as evidence about the second millennium BCE is
the §4.M trap in its plainest form.

**It is not support for the migration model.** `DMG-2` says so in terms and
that note stays. The movement it records runs **westward, out of Pakistan, in
the modern period**. A medieval eastward migration into Balochistan is the
wrong direction and the wrong century. That it would be the most quotable thing
in this unit is exactly why it is refused.

## What is needed

Rights-steward review under `D-041`: consent for use, correct attribution
including of the named informants, and a decision on whether the two
scholar-authored items in community venues fall under the same requirement.
Separately, `D-039` asks for the University of Balochistan hosts on the egress
allowlist — at present these articles are indexed and abstract-readable while
their publisher is unreachable, so even the attribution cannot be checked
against the published text.
