# HOLD-008 — Shortugai, and the excavation-monograph lane generally

**Opened:** 2026-09-09, domain R (materials and corridors).
**Blocks:** the Indus-side node of THE BLUE ROAD entirely; `HYP-R-009`,
which is recorded as `CANNOT-GATE`; and the BMAC component of the same road,
which is reachable here only in passing citation.
**Typed absence:** `DR-NEG-002`, `NOT ACCESSIBLE`.

## What is needed

The excavation record of **Shortugai**, in Badakhshan province, northern
Afghanistan, and the literature interpreting it: principally
**H.-P. Francfort, *Fouilles de Shortughaï*, 1989**, and the subsequent
discussion of the site's relation to lapis procurement, to the Indus
civilisation and to the Bactria-Margiana Archaeological Complex.

Secondarily, the same lane would supply the Oxus civilisation material this
unit could not reach: Sarianidi's and Lyonnet and Dubova's Gonur Depe
publications, of which only a single soft-stone vessel reached this unit and
that through a journal article about Arabia (`SRC-116`).

## Why it matters more than any other gap in this unit

The owner's instruction names Shortugai as a component of THE BLUE ROAD. It is
the one place on that road where the Indus and the lapis source are supposed to
meet on the ground, and it is therefore the site that would bear on every one of
the three claims the domain has to keep apart: whether the material moved,
whether people moved, and whether anything else moved with them. A Blue Road
reconstructed without it runs Badakhshan → Iranian plateau → Mesopotamia and is
silent at exactly the point the programme most needs.

This unit accordingly makes **no claim about Shortugai in either direction**.
It does not claim the site was an Indus outpost; it does not claim it was not.

## What was tried

A Scholar Gateway query of 2026-09-09T02:14Z, framed on the site by name
together with Harappan settlement, Badakhshan lapis and the BMAC, returned
20 passages across 9 articles. **None concerned Shortugai.** The only trace
of it anywhere in the retrieval is `Francfort, 1989` appearing inside a list of
citations for Bronze Age sites in northern Afghanistan, in a paper about
archaeobiology in Central Asia (`SRC-128`).

Direct HTTPS to `persee.fr`, `hal.science`, `journals.openedition.org`,
`archive.org`, `jstor.org` and `cambridge.org` is refused at the egress gateway
with 403 to CONNECT and zero bytes returned (`SRC-099`).

## Why the lane and not just the book

Shortugai is the presenting case, but the hold is on a class. This session's
only literature lane is one publisher's journal catalogue (`SRC-105`), and the
material it cannot reach is not randomly distributed: it is the excavation
monograph, the national-mission series, and the French, Russian and Italian
publications in which most of Central Asian and Iranian Bronze Age archaeology
is published. `BF-027` records that as a method failure of this unit rather than
as a property of the evidence, and the Shortugai query is kept as its
demonstration.

## What would clear it

Any one of:

1. Access to `persee.fr` or `journals.openedition.org`, where French
   archaeological series are digitised — the narrowest sufficient change.
2. The Francfort volumes supplied directly.
3. A literature lane covering Elsevier and the Cambridge catalogue, which would
   also close much of `BF-027` — the broadest, and it overlaps `D-001` and
   `D-055`.

## What must not happen while it is held

No claim about Shortugai may be written from general knowledge. The site is
well known, which makes it exactly the case where an unretrieved recollection
would enter a register wearing the authority of a finding. `BF-031` records the
same discipline being applied to a date.
