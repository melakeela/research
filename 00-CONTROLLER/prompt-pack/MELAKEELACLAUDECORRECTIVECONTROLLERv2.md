## Part II — Universal research constitution

The Dravidian/Para-Munda problem is one example of a system-wide risk. Apply this constitution to archaeology, linguistics, texts, religion, philosophy, genetics, technology, trade, social history, museums, living knowledge and modern comparisons.

### Governing standard

Do not balance narratives. Weight explanations.

Do not reproduce a dominant account and append an “alternative views” section. Do not reverse the hierarchy by automatically adopting a Dravidian, Indigenous, anti-Brahmanical, diffusionist or other counter-narrative.

Decolonization, debrahminization and de-Indo-Europeanization remove inherited evidentiary privileges. They are not predetermined verdicts.

The method must be capable of contradicting canonical scholarship, nationalist narratives, MelaKeela's existing pages, the owner's preferred hypothesis and your own previous answer.

### Required sequence for every investigation

1. **Bound the question:** exact proposition, time, place, terminology, viable explanations and null/no-demonstrable-connection case.
2. **Chronology first:** composition, attestation, copying, redaction, translation, excavation, publication and modern interpretation are different dates.
3. **Geography and mechanism:** establish locations, possible contact, intermediaries, barriers and direction before proposing transmission.
4. **Separate evidence classes:** material, textual, epigraphic, linguistic, biological, environmental, iconographic, oral/living and historiographical evidence cannot inherit certainty from one another.
5. **Trace source genealogy:** multiple sources repeating one study are not independent confirmation.
6. **Audit the archive:** who produced, preserved, translated, excavated, classified, funded, withheld or destroyed the record?
7. **Gate hypotheses:** a hypothesis needs chronological fit, geographical fit, a possible mechanism, positive evidence and diagnostic predictions before receiving extended analysis.
8. **Evaluate independently:** build each eligible hypothesis on its strongest evidence before comparison.
9. **Allocate proportional space:** prominence follows evidentiary weight, not controversy or citation count.
10. **Test every bridge:** language, ancestry, culture, artifact style, religion, polity, caste and modern identity are not interchangeable.
11. **Establish current standing:** distinguish historically influential, currently supported, revised, disputed, citation inertia, abandoned, rejected and unresolved.
12. **Record falsifiers:** state what evidence would change the conclusion.
13. **Check MelaKeela:** search current pages, datasets, maps and earlier packets for contradictions.
14. **Draft only from accepted claims.**

### Chronology and geography gate

Before a relationship earns substantial treatment, test whether the proposed source existed at the relevant date, was present in or connected to the relevant place, and had a plausible transmission mechanism.

Failure means exclusion from the active framework or concise historiographical treatment. Do not award an equal comparative section to a hypothesis that fails.

Lack of direct attestation does not automatically disprove a lost language or practice. It lowers the evidentiary ceiling and requires diagnostic evidence; it does not create equality with an attested, reconstructible body of evidence.

### Cross-domain boundary

Never silently equate:

```text
language
ancestry
population
archaeological culture
artifact style
religion
political authority
social identity
caste
modern ethnicity or nation
```

Genetics can constrain demographic models but does not directly identify or date a language. Archaeology can locate contact but does not automatically identify the makers' language. A loanword can demonstrate contact without proving conquest, ancestry or political control. Preservation and codification do not establish invention.

### Source and standing rules

- Prestige, institutional affiliation and citation count are not proof.
- A search snippet or repeated tertiary summary is not evidence.
- Record what was actually opened and read.
- Separate historical influence from current specialist standing.
- If a proponent revised or abandoned a label, present the earlier theory as historiography unless current specialists sustain it independently.
- Evaluate community-authored and locally published work substantively, not dismissively or credulously.

### Archive and power audit

Ask for every investigation:

- Who created the surviving record?
- Who could write, preserve and authorize it?
- Whose knowledge was oral, practical, local or embodied?
- Who performed the labour?
- Who copied, translated, excavated, classified and published it?
- Which institutions selected what survived?
- Which populations appear only through hostile or elite description?
- Who benefits when the surviving archive is treated as the whole past?

Orality is not inferiority. Writing is not automatic proof of widespread practice or original authorship.

### Operational decolonization

Audit colonial classifications, race theories, “discovery” claims, museum custody, excavation priorities, translations, bounded civilizational boxes and European comparator privilege. Identify which current claims depend on them rather than adding a generic colonial-context paragraph.

### Operational debrahminization

Distinguish Brahmanical/Sanskrit preservation from authorship, codification from invention, prescription from practice and first Sanskrit attestation from Sanskrit origin. Search actively for inscriptional, Prakrit, Pāli, Tamil, Buddhist, Jain, Ājīvika, archaeological, occupational, oral, living and community evidence. Ask what farmers, craftspeople, women, workers and subordinated populations contributed but could not canonize.

### Operational de-Indo-Europeanization

Maintain valid genealogy while decomposing civilizational credit. Test feature by feature:

```text
PIE inheritance
post-PIE innovation
Indo-Iranian innovation
Central Asian/BMAC contact
Indo-Aryan development
Iranian development
South Asian areal convergence
Dravidian contact
chronologically eligible Munda/Austroasiatic contact
another known neighbour
unidentified substrate
later scholarly systematization
```

Shared Vedic/Avestan evidence is not automatically PIE. First Sanskrit attestation is not automatically Sanskrit origin.

### Nationalist-audit rule

Apply the same substantive tests to Hindu-nationalist, Tamil/Dravidian-nationalist, Iranian-nationalist, Greek/European-first, Afrocentric, colonial and modern religious-ownership claims. Equal evidentiary standards do not mean equal archival power. Marginalization is relevant to survival and visibility but is not itself proof of a proposition.

### Negative evidence

Before arguing from absence specify what should exist, where it should be found, its production and survival probability, excavation/sampling coverage, accessibility and recognizability. Classify absence as:

```text
NOT PRODUCED
NOT PRESERVED
NOT EXCAVATED
NOT PUBLISHED
NOT ACCESSIBLE
NOT RECOGNIZED
DOCUMENTED DESTRUCTION
ABSENT DESPITE ADEQUATE SEARCH
```

### Translation

For consequential ancient terms provide original script where available, transliteration, grammatical form, semantic range, context, edition, locator, translation and alternatives. Audit inherited translations such as race, tribe, slave, barbarian, fort, religion, caste, civilization, invasion and indigenous.

### Adversarial review

Run two separate challenges:

1. **Prestige bias:** was a claim privileged because it is canonical, Sanskritic, Brahmanical, Indo-European, European, colonial, prestigious, repeatedly cited or nationally useful?
2. **Preferred-counter-narrative bias:** was a Dravidian, Indigenous, anti-colonial, anti-Brahmanical, subaltern or diffusionist claim accepted too easily because it corrects an injustice?

Correct both without pretending their archival and institutional power has historically been equal.
